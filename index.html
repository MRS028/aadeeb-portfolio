Research Experience

Undergraduate Thesis — Auxetic-Clamping Suppression of Peel-Stress Failure in FDM-PETG Single-Lap Adhesive Joints

Sept 2025 – Aug 2026

Supervisor: Md. Golam Kader | Co-Supervisor: Dr. Md. Ashraful Islam

  • 3D-printed FDM-PETG auxetic-clamping inserts (negative Poisson's ratio geometries) to suppress peel-stress concentration at single-lap adhesive joint overlap edges.
  • Ran a large-scale experimental campaign across design variations; measured failure load and mode; used RF, XGBoost, and Bayesian optimization to improve geometry iteratively based on experimental feedback.
  • Identified key geometric parameters governing peel-stress suppression via SHAP explainability; manuscript under review at Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence (EAAI-26-21519).

LPBF Residual Stress & Distortion Prediction (ML Framework)

July 2026 - Present

Supervisor: Dr. Md Mahbubur Rahman

  • Building a parametric ANSYS FEA study of Ti-6Al-4V LPBF across laser power, scan speed, layer thickness, hatch spacing, and part geometry to generate structured ML training data.
  • Training and benchmarking RF, XGBoost, Gradient Boosting, and ANN models for simultaneous residual stress and distortion prediction; applying SHAP analysis for process parameter sensitivity ranking.

Mechanical Performance of GFRP Epoxy Laminates with Nanoparticle Fillers

October 2024 - Present

Supervisor: Dr. Md. Arifuzzaman

  • Fabricated GFRP laminates with nine fillers (Al₂O₃, CuO, TiO₂, SiO₂, nanoclay, graphene, pearlite, fly ash, MgO) across five loadings (0.1-0.9 wt%).
  • Measured tensile and 3-point flexural properties. Planned microscopy/XRD, fracture-surface analysis.
  • Validated the whole experiment for the second time to ensure reproducibility.

Mechanical Properties Prediction of CFRP Composites with ML and Explainable AI

September 2024 – December 2025

Supervisor: Dr. Md Ashraful Islam

  • Trained RF, XGBoost, Gradient Boost, LightGBM, KNN, SVR, and Decision Tree models on nanoparticle-enhanced CFRP datasets; used SHAP to rank feature importance across tensile and flexural outputs.
  • Identified fiber-layer count as the dominant tensile predictor and curing duration as the key flexural driver; proposed data-guided manufacturing settings for better laminate performance.

Nuclear Energy Research: Social Acceptance & Safety Analysis

July 2024 – January 2026

Supervisor: Dr. Md Abdullah Al Bari

  • Synthesized scholarship on risk perception, institutional trust, social costs, and media dynamics around nuclear power; drew cross-country contrasts (Japan, China, Germany, France, developing nations).
  • Found institutional trust to be a stronger predictor of acceptance than technical risk metrics; proposed communication pathways.
  • Simulated 25-50% steam-line breaks plus 50% turbine trip with PCTRAN (VVER-1200); observed scrams at ~3 s, ~21 s, ~91-95 s; system restabilized ~1000 s — accepted at SCOPE 2025 (KFUPM).
